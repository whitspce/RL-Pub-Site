Stream from your phone to OBS at home

Send your camera feed to a home computer running OBS Studio. Use OBS for scenes, overlays and your final broadcast, with scene and broadcast controls on your phone.

Why set it up this way

Your phone sends its camera and microphone to OBS on your computer at home. OBS turns that into your finished stream and sends it to your platform.

  • Your computer does the heavy lifting. Scenes, overlays and alerts all run at home.
  • If your phone drops out, OBS keeps the stream up. You don't have to pay for a drop protection service.
  • OBS can send your stream to more than one platform.

This guide uses Tailscale, a separate app that connects your phone to your home computer privately, including on mobile data. Other setups can work too.

What you need

  • An Android phone running Android 10 or newer.
  • A computer that can run OBS Studio 28 or newer. It has to stay on and awake while you stream.

1. Install the apps

On your phone:

  • Roam Live from Google Play
  • Tailscale from Google Play

On your computer:

  • OBS Studio
  • Tailscale for Windows, macOS or Linux
New to OBS?

The first time you open OBS it may offer its Auto-Configuration Wizard. You can cancel it for now.

2. Connect your phone and computer with Tailscale

Use the same Tailscale account on both.

Using another VPN? Turn it off on the phone and the computer first. Android only runs one VPN at a time, so Tailscale and another VPN can't be on together.

On your phone

  1. Open Tailscale and tap Get Started.
  2. Android asks to set up a VPN connection. Allow it. Android shows this warning for every VPN app.
  3. Sign in. If Tailscale opens your browser for this, go back to the Tailscale app when you're done.
  4. Check that Tailscale says it is connected.

On your computer

  1. Open Tailscale and sign in with the same account.
  2. Check that it says it is connected.
Can't find Tailscale on your computer?

On Windows, Tailscale is an icon near the clock. If you can't see it, click the small up arrow there. Right-click the icon and choose Log in.

On a Mac, open Tailscale from Applications and follow its setup. It asks you to allow a VPN configuration. After that it sits in the menu bar at the top of the screen.

Linux: connect from the terminal

There may be no app icon. Open a terminal and run:

tailscale version
sudo tailscale up --timeout=5m
tailscale ip -4

The first line checks that Tailscale is installed. The second connects it and prints a sign-in link if one is needed. If sudo asks for a password, type your computer login password. You won't see it as you type. The last line shows the computer's Tailscale address.

The connect command gives up after five minutes. If no sign-in link appears, press Ctrl+C, turn off any other VPN, check that normal browsing works and try again.

Can't see your other device?

Check that both devices are signed in to the same Tailscale account and both show as connected. Leave Exit node at None. If you use another VPN, see Tailscale's notes on other VPNs.

Tailscale's own Android steps may help too.

Find your computer's Tailscale address

Open Tailscale on the computer. It shows this computer's address, which starts with 100. You need it in the next step. It is also listed beside your computer's name in the Tailscale app on your phone.

3. Fill in your details

Type these in once. The page builds the two addresses you need. Nothing you type leaves this page.

Starts with 100.

Letters and numbers only. At least 20.

Keep it somewhere safe. OBS and Roam must use the same one.

Replace the two coloured parts. Use letters and numbers only in the passphrase, at least 20. Leave everything else exactly as it is. OBS and Roam must use the same passphrase.

For OBS (step 4)

srt://YOUR-COMPUTER-ADDRESS:1234?mode=listener&passphrase=YOUR-PASSPHRASE&pbkeylen=32&latency=2000000

For Roam (step 5)

srt://YOUR-COMPUTER-ADDRESS:1234/live?passphrase=YOUR-PASSPHRASE&pbkeylen=256&latency=2000

Setting up the phone? Open this page on the phone too, type the same two details, and copy the Roam address there.

Why two different addresses?

OBS waits for the phone, and Roam calls it. Each app writes the same settings in its own way, so the two addresses look different. Use each one only where it belongs.

4. Set up OBS to receive your phone

  1. Open OBS. In Sources, click + and choose Media (called Media Source in older versions). Name it Roam phone.
  2. Untick Local File.
  3. Paste the OBS address from step 3 into Input.
  4. Type mpegts into Input Format.
  5. Set Reconnect Delay to 1 second if you see that setting.
  6. Untick Restart playback when source becomes active. Leave Close file when inactive unticked.
  7. Leave the other settings as they are and click OK.

The source stays blank until Roam starts sending. That's normal.

5. Set up Roam

  1. Open Roam Live and allow the camera and microphone when it asks.
  2. Open Settings, then Stream.
  3. Paste the Roam address from step 3 into Stream URL.
Roam says the stream destination isn't valid

Check that there are no spaces in the address and no < or > signs left in it. The passphrase must be letters and numbers only. Tap the eye icon in the Stream URL box to see the whole address.

6. Optional: control OBS from your phone

Skip this if you only want your camera in OBS. Do it if you want to switch scenes or start and stop the OBS broadcast from Roam.

In OBS:

  1. Open Tools, then WebSocket Server Settings.
  2. Make sure Enable WebSocket server is ticked.
  3. Make sure Enable Authentication is ticked. If Server Password is empty, click Generate Password.
  4. Click OK.
  5. Open WebSocket Server Settings again, click Show Connect Info and note the Server Password.

In Roam, open Settings, then OBS, and fill in:

FieldWhat to enter
Host (IP address or hostname)Your computer's Tailscale address. Use it even if OBS shows a different Server IP.
Port4455
Password (required)The Server Password from OBS

Then:

  1. Tap Connect and wait for Connected.
  2. Turn on Show OBS control on main screen. An OBS pill now shows at the top of Roam's camera screen.

7. Test it

  1. In Roam, hold Go live. Your phone's picture should show up in OBS within a few seconds.
  2. Talk. On the computer, the bar beside Roam phone in OBS's Audio Mixer panel should move.
  3. If you did step 6, tap the OBS pill at the top of Roam's camera screen. It opens the OBS controls. Tap a scene to switch to it.
  4. Streaming on mobile data? Turn off Wi-Fi on the phone, check Tailscale is still connected, and try again before you leave home.

Going live in Roam only sends your camera to OBS. Nobody is watching yet. Your viewers see the stream once OBS is streaming.

Can't see the Audio Mixer in OBS?

Open the Docks menu at the top of OBS and tick Audio Mixer.

Picture is the wrong size

Right-click the Roam phone source and choose Transform > Fit to screen.

Can't hear the phone on the computer

OBS doesn't play the phone's sound through your speakers unless you turn on audio monitoring. That's separate from what goes into your stream. To check the sound, make a short recording with Start Recording and play it back. See the OBS Audio Mixer guide.

8. Go live from OBS

  1. In OBS, open Settings, then Stream. Pick your platform under Service. Connect your account, or paste the stream key your platform gives you into Stream Key.
  2. Hold Go live in Roam so your camera is in OBS.
  3. Click Start Streaming in OBS. If you did step 6 you can start it from Roam: tap the OBS pill at the top of Roam's camera screen and use Hold to start OBS broadcast.
  4. To finish, stop the OBS broadcast first (Stop Streaming in OBS, or Stop OBS broadcast in Roam). Then stop Roam.

Put your phone in every scene

Add the same Roam phone source to every scene you switch to, even scenes that don't show it. In each scene, click + in Sources, choose Media and add the existing Roam phone. Where you don't want to see it, place it underneath your other sources instead of hiding it. If a scene doesn't have it, OBS can restart the feed when you switch back, and the picture can come back delayed.

Extras

Break screen (BRB)

This needs step 6, with Roam connected to OBS. Make an OBS scene for your break screen, with the Roam phone source underneath your break image.

In Roam, open Settings, then OBS. Under BRB scene in OBS, choose that scene. Leave Mute mic and camera during OBS BRB on.

Roam's BRB button now switches OBS to that scene. While it is live, Roam sends a black picture and no sound instead of your camera and microphone.

Start and stop the OBS broadcast from Roam

This needs step 6. Tap the OBS pill at the top of Roam's camera screen. In the OBS controls, Hold to start OBS broadcast starts OBS streaming to your platform and Stop OBS broadcast stops it. They work even when Roam isn't sending your camera. They don't start or stop OBS recording.

Sync OBS streaming with Roam is off unless you turn it on. With it on, holding Go live also starts the OBS broadcast, straight away and without waiting for you to check the picture. When you stop Roam, it only stops an OBS broadcast that it started.

Test on Twitch without going live

Twitch has a test mode that takes your stream without showing it to viewers.

  1. In OBS, open Profile, then Duplicate, and name the copy Twitch Bandwidth Test.
  2. In Settings, then Stream, choose Twitch. If you connected your account, tick Enable Bandwidth Test Mode. If you use a stream key, add ?bandwidthtest=true to the end of it in Stream Key: 
    YOUR-STREAM-KEY?bandwidthtest=true
  3. Open Twitch Inspector, start streaming in OBS and check that Inspector sees the test. Also check your channel in a signed-out browser. If it shows as live, stop OBS and fix the setup.
  4. When you're done, stop OBS and switch back to your normal profile.

Twitch documents that the bandwidth test setting turns off live viewing. It only works on Twitch.

Stream to several platforms

Get your first platform working first. Your phone still sends one feed to OBS either way.

Through a restreaming service

OBS sends one stream to the service, and the service passes it on to each platform. See the free services and their limits.

For Restream, connect your platform accounts there and turn on the channels you want. In OBS, open Settings, then Stream, choose Restream.io and connect your account, or use the stream key Restream gives you. Restream's OBS setup has the details. Roam's OBS controls start and stop this broadcast. Check that each platform shows as live.

Straight from OBS with a free plugin

Multiple RTMP outputs adds more destinations to OBS. Each one is a separate upload from your home internet, so make sure it can handle the total.

  1. Stop streaming and close OBS. Install the plugin from its official releases, picking the build for your system and OBS version. A plugin can need a newer OBS than this guide does. Open OBS again.
  2. Keep your first platform in Settings, then Stream. Open Docks > Multiple output and choose Add new target.
  3. Name it and enter that platform's server address and stream key in their own fields.
  4. For video and audio, choose Reuse the streaming encoder as OBS if the platforms accept the same settings. It saves work on the computer. Each platform is still its own upload.
  5. Turn on both Sync start with OBS and Sync stop with OBS so the target follows your main OBS broadcast. Save it. Repeat for any other platforms.
  6. At the computer, start Roam and the OBS broadcast. Check picture, sound and live status on every platform. Stop the broadcast and check that every platform stops.
  7. Before you rely on it away from home, do the same start and stop with Roam's OBS controls.

The two sync settings are off for new targets, so check them. Roam controls the main OBS broadcast and can't tell whether each extra target is live. Extra targets are real broadcasts. Twitch's test mode only applies where you set it.

Less delay

The addresses use a two-second buffer. It smooths over a patchy connection and it is why OBS shows your phone slightly late. You can try a smaller number, for example 1000 in the Roam address and 1000000 in the OBS address. Change both. A smaller buffer breaks up more easily on mobile data.

Why Tailscale, and router ports

Tailscale connects your phone to your home computer while you're out. It works through most home routers without changing any settings, and it encrypts everything between your two devices. That keeps your camera feed and your OBS controls private, so nobody else can watch the feed or switch your scenes.

You don't need to open any ports on your router. If you opened ports 1234 or 4455 for an older version of this guide, close them. Don't share OBS through Tailscale Funnel either, because that makes it public.

How the three connections fit together
ConnectionWhat it doesWhere you set it up
Roam to OBS, using SRTSends your phone's picture and soundRoam Settings > Stream and an OBS media source (steps 4 and 5)
Roam to OBS WebSocketSwitches scenes and starts or stops the OBS broadcastRoam Settings > OBS (step 6)
OBS to your platformSends the finished stream to viewersOBS Settings > Stream (step 8)

The OBS controls don't carry your camera. Sending your camera to OBS doesn't start the OBS broadcast unless Sync OBS streaming with Roam is on.

Troubleshooting

Roam says the stream destination isn't valid

Stream destination contains invalid characters means there is a space in the address, even at the very start or end. Stream destination is not valid usually means a character that can't go in an address, like < or >, or a symbol in the passphrase. SRT passphrase must be 10 to 79 characters means the passphrase is too short or too long.

Copy the Roam address from step 3 again and paste it over the old one. To see the whole address in Roam, tap the eye icon in the Stream URL box.

OBS shows nothing from the phone

Check that Tailscale is connected on both devices. Check that each app has its own address from step 3 and that both use the same passphrase. Check that Input Format is mpegts, the Roam phone source's eye icon is on, and no other source covers it. In Studio Mode, viewers see the Program side. OBS has a guide to receiving SRT.

The OBS control and the video are separate connections. A working OBS control doesn't mean the video is getting through.

OBS doesn't pick up the phone after a restart

Wait a few seconds after stopping Roam before you start it again. OBS needs a moment to reopen. Setting Reconnect Delay to 1 second shortens the wait.

The OBS control is missing from Roam

Turn on Show OBS control on main screen in Roam's Settings > OBS. Hiding the control doesn't disconnect OBS or turn off sync.

Roam can't connect to OBS

Check that Tailscale is connected on both devices, OBS is open and the computer is awake. Check the address, port 4455, the password, and that Enable Authentication is ticked in OBS. Put only the address in Host (IP address or hostname), without http://, ws://, a port or a path. See OBS's remote control guide.

The picture stops when you switch scenes

Put the same Roam phone source in every scene, as in Put your phone in every scene. Check that Restart playback when source becomes active and Close file when inactive are both unticked. OBS explains these in its media source guide.

The audio bar moves, but the recording is silent

Check that the Roam phone source isn't muted in OBS and is on the audio track your recording or stream uses. If its monitoring is set to Monitor Only (mute output), it's left out of your stream and recordings. Don't also pick up the same sound through Desktop Audio or a second mic, or you'll get an echo.

Mobile data is choppy

In Roam, try Data saver under Video quality, with Auto bitrate on. Check the home internet too. When Tailscale can't connect your devices directly, it sends traffic through a relay, and that can be too slow for steady video. See Tailscale's page on direct and relayed connections.

The feed and normal internet both stop

Stop Roam and OBS, then check normal internet on both devices. Use a network cable for the home computer if you can. Try a short test on mobile data before going back to higher quality.

A firewall blocks the connection

The video uses UDP port 1234 and the OBS controls use TCP port 4455, between your two devices over Tailscale. Allow only that on the computer's firewall, and check any Tailscale access rules. Don't turn the firewall off or open ports on your router. Tailscale's connection troubleshooting can help.

Tailscale is off and the phone has no internet

Android's Always-on VPN or Block connections without VPN may be waiting for a VPN that isn't connected. This setup doesn't need either, or an exit node. Reconnect Tailscale and check that normal browsing works.

Still stuck? Get in touch and say which step failed, plus your Roam version, phone model, computer system and OBS version. Remove passwords, full stream addresses and account details from screenshots and logs first.
